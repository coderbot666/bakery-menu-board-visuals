Bakery Menu Board Visuals: Notes from a Small Shop

These are working notes from refreshing the menu board and window posters for a neighbourhood bakery. The budget was close to zero, the deadline was a long weekend, and the photos had to look like our actual bread.

Start from real photos

Every item was photographed on the counter in morning light from the front window. AI tools were only used to clean up backgrounds and build seasonal variations, never to invent pastries we do not bake. Customers compare the board with the display case, so any mismatch shows immediately.

One background, many items

We picked a single warm wooden surface and asked the editor to place each item on it, keeping the shape and colour of the product unchanged. A shared background is what makes twelve separate photos read as one menu.

Seasonal swaps

For autumn we asked for the same scene with a few fallen leaves and a slightly warmer light; for the holidays, a sprig of pine and a dusting of flour. Because the products stayed the same, the variations took minutes. Most of this pass was done in Nano Banana 2, which held the loaves and croissants steady while the props around them changed.

Text stays in the layout

Prices and names change weekly, so none of the text is baked into the images. The pictures leave clean space at the bottom, and the menu software lays the words on top.

What we would do differently

  • Shoot everything on one morning so the light matches from the start.
  • Keep a plain-background version of every item for the website.
  • Print one test poster before ordering the full run; colours shift on paper.

A small shop does not need a studio for a good menu board, but it does need the food in the pictures to be the food on the shelf.
